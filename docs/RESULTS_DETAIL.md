# Scam Drift: detailed results

**post-deadline v2 (Nov 18).** The hackathon entry was judged at git commit `01abbc9` on `main`; that commit does not change. This document describes the post-deadline branch. Part A holds the v2 additions (docs/V2_SPEC.md items 1-10). Part B is the v1 text of the judged commit; its numbers are reproduced unchanged by the v1 keys of `results/metrics.json`.

Every number below is read from `results/metrics.json` (key `v2` for Part A) or the CSV tables in `results/`, from the seeded run of `src/scam_drift.py` (seed 42, runtime 125.4 s on CPU). This document is generated by `tools/write_results_detail.py`. "95% CI" means a Wilson score interval. Rates on fewer than 1,000 messages always carry a CI.

Labels used throughout: the benign twins are a **synthetic stress test, written with AI assistance**. Non-English results on English translations are an **oracle upper reference** (the translation came with the dataset; no translator was run). Years are **report-date cohorts** (the year visible in the reporter's screenshot), not send dates.

This project is a prototype and is not financial advice.

# Part A: v2 additions (post-deadline v2 (Nov 18))

## A0. Summary

- The naive fix (`word_fixed`) catches 0.9657 of 2022-24 scams. It also flags 30/30 = 1.0 (0.8865-1.0) held-out synthetic legitimate texts and 605/608 = 0.9951 (0.9856-0.9983) real legitimate India SMS from held-out sender groups. Its recall is therefore not a win.

- Adding 30 synthetic legitimate texts (`fix_plus_benign`) does not help on the 30 held-out synthetic texts from other categories: 30/30 = 1.0 (0.8865-1.0).

- Adding 598 real legitimate SMS (`fix_plus_real_ham`) lowers false alarms on held-out India groups from 0.9951 to 399/608 = 0.6562 (0.6176-0.6929), and on the untouched Mozambique set from 0.625 to 0.5305, with 2022-24 recall 0.9481. False alarms on real modern legitimate texts remain high at the cost-based threshold.

- Model and threshold rule chosen on validation data only (the tier design is post-hoc): the prototype configuration is `char_fix_plus_real_ham` with the `fa_budget_10pct_oof` threshold (0.94) inside the three-tier triage. On 2022-24 scams: RED 427/1,080 = 0.3954 (0.3666-0.4249), RED or YELLOW 709/1,080 = 0.6565 (0.6276-0.6842). On held-out real India legit SMS: RED 71/608 = 0.1168 (0.0936-0.1447), RED or YELLOW 139/608 = 0.2286 (0.197-0.2636).

- Filter half-life: the 2012 model's recall on report-date cohorts is roughly flat from 2017 to 2023 (linear slope -0.0117 per year, cohorts with n >= 100). Half-life: not reached: no report-date cohort (n >= 30) falls to half of the time-zero recall. The gap is an era/dataset shift between 2012 SMS and 2020s reports, not a smooth yearly decline.

## A1. Source confound (item 1)

Source-separation AUC (word TF-IDF + logistic regression, 5-fold CV, after normalisation). Near 1.0 means the two sources are easy to tell apart.

| Comparison | n per side | CV ROC AUC |
|---|---|---|
| UCI spam vs modern scam (v1 `shortcut_check`) | 619 / 3000 | 0.9893 |
| UCI ham vs real India legit SMS | 1206 / 1206 | 0.999 |
| Real India legit SMS vs modern scam | 1206 / 1206 | 0.9884 |

Reading: legitimate texts are at least as easy to tell apart by source as scams are (UCI ham vs India legit AUC 0.999). A model trained with 2012 ham as the only negatives can learn "modern = scam".

`fix_plus_benign`: training negatives = UCI ham + the synthetic legit texts of 30 rows from the categories bank_alert, bank_otp, utility_other (seeded, phrasing-disjoint split by category). Test-only: 30 legit texts from delivery, medical, pharmacy and all 40 scam twins. Results for every model are in section A9.

## A8. Threshold discipline (item 8)

Rule: each threshold is chosen on out-of-fold predictions of the model's own training data (5 folds; group-aware when India rows are in training), minimising 5 x missed scams + 1 x false alarms; frozen before any 2022+ or held-out evaluation. Cost ratio missed scam : false alarm = 5.0:1. Alternative rule: false-alarm budget: lowest threshold with <= 10% out-of-fold false alarms on India-train legit.

## A11. Limitations (v2)

- The India set is one person's inbox; brand-or-template grouping reduces but does not remove shared style. Treat its rates as optimistic about representativeness.
- Mozambique rates mix language shift with time shift. Both real sets were looked at during exploration before these numbers were final.
- The synthetic legit set is small (60; 30 held out) and written with AI assistance.
- The 5% scam rate behind the prevalence-adjusted precision and the selection cost is an assumption, not a measurement.
- The selection validation (2021 scams, one half of the India training groups) is small; a different seed could pick a different variant.
- The tiers, the rules and the strict threshold were designed after the v1 results were seen (post-hoc); they were then frozen and evaluated.
- Report-date cohorts are not send dates, and their scam-type and language mix changes by year.

**Time split decision.** The IMC25 `time` field is the raw timestamp from the reporter's screenshot ("Today 8:15 AM", "Tuesday 7 March 12:52"). We date a row only if the string shows a four-digit year (2015-2025) or a d/m/yy date. English dated rows per year (after dedupe; `results/recall_by_year.csv`): 2015: 1, 2016: 3, 2017: 126, 2018: 175, 2019: 284, 2020: 521, 2021: 699 (train pool total 1,809), 2022: 694, 2023: 378, 2024: 8 (test total 1,080). A cut at the end of 2021 gives both sides more than 1,000 rows. A cut at the end of 2022 would leave fewer than 400 test rows.

**The dated subset is not a random sample.** Only about 16% of IMC25 rows (5,295 of 33,869) show a year, and which timestamp formats show a year depends on the reporter's phone and locale. The year parser also misses some rows: strings with a 2010-2014 year and forms such as "Mon 15th May '23" are treated as undated. These rows go to the undated evaluation set, never to training, so this causes no leakage.

**Dedupe.** Exact duplicates after normalisation are removed, keeping the earliest dated copy, so a campaign text never appears on both sides of the split.

## B2. Shortcut check
A word TF-IDF + logistic regression classifier separates UCI spam (n=619) from a 3,000-row sample of IMC25 English scams after normalisation with 5-fold CV ROC AUC **0.9893** (balanced accuracy 0.9454).

- Tokens that say "IMC25": `tok_date`, `please`, `tok_url`, `account`, `card`, `your`, `at`, `dear`, `bank`, `payment`, `of tok_money`, `dear customer`, `here`, `has`, `thank`, `pay`, `click`, `details`, `for tok_date`, `thank you`.

**Threshold choice.** Each threshold minimises 5 x missed scams + 1 x false alarms on 5-fold out-of-fold predictions of the model's own training data. Why 5x: a missed scam can lead to an irreversible loss of savings; a false alarm costs a short check with the bank or family, but frequent false alarms teach the user to ignore warnings, so we did not choose a much larger ratio. Caveat: the fixed model's validation data is 2,304 scams out of 5,917 messages, far more scams than a real inbox, which pushes its threshold down (0.24).

**Link gate (post-hoc, exploratory).** Warn only if the model flags the message AND it contains a link. This was added after the first results were seen, so it is not a pre-registered model.
